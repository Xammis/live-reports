Research snapshot / 01 October 2026

More human.
Less generic.
More useful.

Recent graphic, brand, web and UI/UX signals, plus a watchlist for late 2026–2027. Not a list of fashionable effects to paste onto every project.

The shared direction:
distinctive expression,
clearer interaction.

Creative reports emphasize tactile, imperfect and culturally specific expression. Web examples emphasize ownable visual systems, edited copy and better wayfinding. AI is changing how prototypes are built and, more tentatively, how interfaces respond.

“Recent” here means the 2025–2026 source window. Annual trend forecasts are evidence of industry interest, not proof that an entire market adopted a style. The outlook below is Jony’s interpretation, not a verified 2027 trend report.

Giorgia Lupi’s actual working sketches

01 / Recent signals

What is showing up now

The strongest common thread is a reaction against sameness. But expressive maximalism and calm minimalism are both present: the audience and context determine which makes sense. The real-samples gallery above connects selected principles to documented work; it is not a sample for every trend or evidence of future adoption.

Graphic & brand design

G01 Reported signal

Human imperfection & tactile surfaces

Paper, grain, hand-drawn marks, collage and material-like depth make digital work feel less sterile. Canva’s “Notes App Chic” and “Texture Check” overlap with Adobe’s organic, imperfect and sensory themes.

Try: one tactile accent or a real behind-the-scenes image. Avoid: distressing every element or reducing text contrast. A handmade look does not establish that an image is authentic.

[1] Canva · [2] Adobe
G02 Reported signal

Typography carries the personality

Oversized, playful, distorted or hand-rendered display letters act as the main graphic rather than an accessory. Adobe explicitly highlights exaggerated type; Canva reports interest in bold type and poster aesthetics.

Try: one expressive headline with a quiet, readable body. Avoid: treating extreme type distortion as a substitute for a clear message.

[1] · [2]
G03 Reported countertrend

Calm editorial design

Serifs, restrained palettes, generous space and deliberate hierarchy provide a counterweight to visual overload. Canva’s “Explorecore” and “Opt-Out Era” show why simplicity is still relevant, rather than something maximalism has replaced.

Try: editorial hierarchy for longer explanations and premium positioning. Avoid: tiny gray type, empty layouts or removing the information needed to decide.

[1] Canva
G04 Reported signal

Surreal imagery & controlled drama

Unexpected scale, dreamlike combinations and cinematic framing turn an ordinary subject into a memorable concept. Canva’s “Reality Warp” and “Drama Club” align with Adobe’s surreal and immersive directions.

Try: a clearly conceptual campaign visual. Avoid: presenting invented locations, customers, product capabilities or results as documentary evidence.

[1] · [2]
G05 Regional signal

Local culture, not generic nostalgia

Regional lettering, craft, familiar symbols and community stories offer specificity. Canva distinguishes signals in India, Mexico and Spain; Adobe also highlights local and cultural flavor. These are not interchangeable global styles.

Try: work with local creators and culturally accurate references. Avoid: borrowing decorative motifs without understanding their meaning or ownership.

[1] · [2]

Web, UI & UX design

W01 Published examples

Ownable visual systems

Custom illustration, signature interactions and deliberate art direction differentiate otherwise similar sites. Webflow documents examples such as Anthropic’s illustration language and Rootly’s art-framed product UI.

Try: one repeatable illustration or motion rule across touchpoints. Avoid: copying another brand’s recognizable treatment. Examples are linked in the source; their current pages may change.

[3] Webflow examples
W02 Published examples

Short copy, summary first

A clear promise and a scannable overview let visitors understand the offer before choosing detail. Webflow separates “minimalism in copy” from the “TL;DR experience,” citing Ruul and Flabbergast as examples.

Try: a concise overview, concrete proof and optional deeper sections. Avoid: stripping out pricing context, limitations or other decision-critical information.

[3] Webflow
W03 Platform direction

Material depth & translucent chrome

Apple introduced Liquid Glass in June 2025 as a cross-platform material for controls and navigation. This is a real product-design direction, not evidence that every website should become glassmorphic.

Try: restrained depth where layering communicates context. Avoid: transparent text surfaces over unpredictable images, excessive blur or copying native behavior without testing.

[4] Apple · [9] WCAG
W04 Published examples

Wayfinding & meaningful motion

Progress indicators, section markers and targeted type treatments help people understand where they are. Webflow describes guided scrolling as navigation, not just a cinematic scroll story, and documents Emons and Habito examples.

Try: section navigation and short feedback on user actions. Avoid: scroll hijacking, hiding text until animation finishes or forced motion. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

[3] Webflow · [9] WCAG
W05 Workflow capability

AI-assisted, runnable prototypes

Figma Make’s launch demonstrates moving existing designs into interactive prototypes through prompts. The practical shift is testing behavior earlier, not merely generating more static screens.

Try: a small flow with real error, loading and empty states. Avoid: assuming generated code is accessible, secure or production-ready. AI-assisted design is not the same as runtime generative UI.

[5] Figma · [7] NN/g
A useful reality check.

Canva reports DIY/scrapbook search interest up 90% year over year, while clean-layout/serif/simple-branding interest rose 54%. Opposing aesthetics can grow at the same time. These are Canva platform measures, not global adoption or conversion statistics. [1]

02 / Late 2026–2027 watchlist

What may develop next

These are forecasts grounded in the signals above. Confidence describes the direction, not a guaranteed adoption date, business result or percentage of the market.

Forecast / higher confidence

Human-led, AI-assisted art direction

Expect more workflows where AI produces variations while designers control composition, distinctiveness and factual accuracy. The visual language may become more tactile or culturally specific, not more uniformly futuristic.

Basis: overlapping Canva/Adobe themes and working AI-prototyping capabilities. This predicts continued importance, not universal stylistic convergence. [1] [2] [5]

Watch for: teams retaining editable sources, references and explicit art-direction rules rather than accepting the first generated output.

Forecast / moderate confidence

Conversation plus task-specific controls

More assistants may return comparison cards, forms, maps and actionable options alongside text. Google’s A2UI launch provides a concrete early implementation signal, including a restaurant-booking UI.

The likely near-term design opportunity is bounded, useful task interfaces, not replacing every screen with a chat box. Adoption across ordinary marketing sites remains uncertain. [6]

Watch for: confirmation, cancellation, visible action status and an ordinary manual route when AI misunderstands.

Forecast / moderate confidence

Design systems become AI guardrails

As agents assemble interfaces, designers may spend more effort defining approved components, allowable combinations and stable navigation. A2UI’s trusted component catalog is a practical signal; NN/g explains the broader outcome-oriented design idea.

Fully personalized, constantly changing UI is still a lower-confidence horizon. NN/g flags consistency, privacy and technical constraints, and gives no firm adoption timeline. [6] [7]

Watch for: reusable components that preserve labels, accessibility and brand rules, with limits on what an agent can change.

Forecast / moderate confidence

Provenance becomes a design surface

As synthetic media becomes easier to make, showing origin and editing history may become more important in creative handoffs and interfaces. Content Credentials already provides a provenance indicator and inspection model.

My forecast is increased emphasis on communicating evidence, not guaranteed audience trust. Provenance can explain a file’s history; it does not prove a claim is true, and missing credentials do not prove a fake. [8]

Watch for: usable source disclosures, rights records and original photography where factual credibility matters.

03 / The practical filter

Borrow the principle.
Not the costume.

Audience, brand rules and the task come first. A useful trend improves understanding, recognition or emotional relevance without creating a new usability problem.

Adopt as fundamentals

Clarity & reliable behavior

  • Edited copy and a clear reading order.
  • Keyboard access, labels and visible focus.
  • Readable type, contrast and mobile reflow.
  • Real proof and explicit limitations.
Experiment selectively

Distinctiveness

  • A tactile accent or custom illustration.
  • Expressive display type, where brand-approved.
  • Brief, useful motion with a reduced-motion version.
  • A runnable prototype before full implementation.
Treat cautiously

Novelty that adds friction

  • Glass everywhere, especially beneath text.
  • Chaotic layouts for complex operational information.
  • Changing navigation on every AI response.
  • Synthetic visuals masquerading as evidence.
Accessibility is a requirement, not a fashionable look.

WebAIM’s February 2026 automated audit detected WCAG failures on 95.9% of sampled home pages, up from 94.8% in 2025. That is evidence of a persistent problem, not improving adoption. Automated checks detect only part of accessibility; manual and assistive-technology testing remain necessary. [10] [9]

A small experiment worth doing

This is a suggested exercise, not work already performed:

  1. Keep one offer and audience fixed. Build a clear baseline and two alternatives: human-texture and editorial-restraint. Keep key facts and the main action consistent.
  2. Test understanding first. Ask target users what the offer is, why they should believe it and what they would do next. Treat a handful of sessions as qualitative feedback, not statistical proof.
  3. Test usability on mobile and keyboard. Check contrast, labels, focus, reading order and any animation.
  4. Measure the real outcome if deployed. Use an appropriately designed experiment for completed enquiries or task completion, not just clicks. Do not declare a winner without enough evidence.

Method & scope.

Desk research, comparing vendor trend reports with named web examples, first-party product announcements, accessibility evidence and conceptual UX guidance. Sources were accessed on 1 October 2026. Most annual forecasts were published early in the cycle, so this is not a late-2026 market census or an exhaustive global review. Graphic/brand and web/UI/UX are covered; interiors, architecture, fashion and industrial design are outside scope.

No original user research, systematic site sampling, market-size estimate or conversion test was conducted. Reported percentages retain their original population and context. Practical recommendations and confidence labels are Jony’s synthesis. The real-samples gallery uses small published image excerpts with credits and source links, not generated substitutes. Human authorship is checked against published process descriptions and studio/team credits, not inferred from appearance. No forensic AI-use audit was conducted.
